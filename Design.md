# Am I Exam-Ready? Readiness Check Design

Nov 17, 2026 · @Bishal

## Overview

The check has 3 sections and 15 questions: 5 per section.

- **Sections:** Vocabulary, Grammar, Reading
- **Per section:** 1 easy, 2 medium, 2 hard
- **Easy and medium:** typed answers
- **Hard:** multiple choice with three answer options plus "I'm not sure"
- **Language:** instructions in English, questions in German

One easy question is enough for a warm-up and a basics check. The medium questions tell us the most: they are typed, so luck cannot help, and they sit right at exam level. The two hard ones separate "ready" from "almost ready."

## Scoring per section

Each section is scored out of 100%. The ready line is 60%, the same as the real Goethe exam.

| Question | Count | Answer type | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Easy | 1 | Typed | 15% |
| Medium | 2 | Typed | 20% each |
| Hard | 2 | Multiple choice | 22.5% each |

**Marking on hard questions**

- Right: +22.5%
- "I'm not sure": 0
- Wrong: −11.25%

**Key outcomes**

| Result | Score | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basics only (easy + both medium), "I'm not sure" on both hard | 55% | Almost ready |
| Basics plus one hard right, "I'm not sure" on the other | 77.5% | Ready |
| Basics plus one hard right, wrong guess on the other | 66.25% | Ready |
| Everything right | 100% | Ready |

## Why negative marking, and why only on hard questions

Negative marking stops lucky guesses from making a learner look ready.

- **Typed questions need no penalty.** You cannot guess a German word by luck.
- **Hard questions can be guessed.** With three options, a blind guess is right one time in three. Without a penalty, guessing only adds points and could push someone past 60% by luck. That is the exact failure we want to prevent: booking the exam while not actually ready.
- **The penalty cancels out guessing.** A third of the time a guess earns +22.5, and two thirds of the time it costs −11.25. On average that comes to zero. So guessing gains nothing, and "I'm not sure" is the honest, safe choice.
- **"I'm not sure" also tells us something.** It shows which skills the learner knows they are weak in, and that feeds the 2–3 practice tips.

The real exam has no negative marking. This is on purpose: the check measures how ready someone is, it does not copy the exam. The score should measure what you know, not how lucky you are.

## Question structure

One example per difficulty level in each section. Instructions are in English, questions in German.

**Rule: no answer is ever a number.** Numbers are easy to guess, and they can be copied straight from the text without understanding any German, so they show nothing. Every answer is a word.

### Vocabulary

- **Easy (typed):** "Ich trinke gern \_\_\_ (coffee)." Answer: Kaffee
- **Medium (typed):** "Der Zug fährt um 8 Uhr vom \_\_\_ ab (train station)." Answer: Bahnhof
- **Hard (choose):** "Sie möchten eine Briefmarke kaufen. Wohin gehen Sie?" Options: zur Post, zur Apotheke, zum Bahnhof, I'm not sure. Answer: zur Post

### Grammar

- **Easy (typed):** "Ich \_\_\_ in Kolkata (wohnen)." Answer: wohne
- **Medium (typed):** "Er \_\_\_ gut Deutsch (sprechen)." Answer: spricht. It is harder because the vowel changes.
- **Hard (choose):** Which sentence is correct? "Morgen ich fahre nach Berlin", "Morgen fahre ich nach Berlin", "Morgen fahre nach Berlin ich", or I'm not sure. Answer: the second one

### Reading

- **Easy (typed):** A sign says "Apotheke: Montag bis Freitag geöffnet. Am Wochenende geschlossen." Question: "An welchem Tag ist die Apotheke geschlossen? Am \_\_\_" Answer: Samstag or Sonntag. The answer is not in the text, so the learner has to know that "Wochenende" means the weekend.
- **Medium (typed):** "Hallo Anna, ich komme am Freitag. Kannst du Brot kaufen? Tom." What should Anna buy? Answer: Brot
- **Hard (choose):** "Hallo Mehmet, ich bin krank und kann heute nicht zum Kurs kommen. Lisa." What is Lisa doing today? Options: going to the course, staying home, doing homework, I'm not sure. Answer: staying home

## Overall verdict

The three section scores are averaged equally, so each section counts for one third of the final score.

| Overall score | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| 60% and above | Ready |
| About 50% to 60% | Almost ready |
| Below 50% | Not ready |

The weakest section is named first as the thing to practise.

**Weak-section warning:** the verdict always follows the overall score. If any section scores below 40%, the result adds a warning instead of changing the verdict. Example: Vocabulary 100%, Reading 80%, Grammar 20% averages 66.7%, so the result is "Ready, but grammar is a real gap. Practise it before you book."

**How results are shown:** the overall score as a percentage, and each section as questions right, for example "Grammar: 1 of 5 right". It is exact, easy to understand, and shows the data as it is.

**Why bands instead of a precise number:** each section has only five questions, so one answer moves a section score by 15 to 34 points. A low section could be real weakness or one bad moment.

**Example:** Vocabulary 46.25%, Grammar 66.25%, Reading 57.5%. Overall: 170 ÷ 3 = 56.7%. Verdict: Almost ready, work on vocabulary first.

## Checking typed answers

Typed answers are checked leniently, except in grammar.

1. **Capitals are ignored.** "kaffee" counts as "Kaffee". The real exam forgives this too: in the official model test, a writing sample starting "mein name ist" still scored full marks, because writing is marked on whether the task got done.
2. **Umlaut spellings are accepted.** "ae", "oe", "ue" and "ss" count as "ä", "ö", "ü" and "ß", since many keyboards lack those letters.
3. **Extra spaces and punctuation are ignored.**
4. **One wrong letter is allowed in vocabulary and reading.** "Kafee" counts as "Kaffee": the learner clearly knows the word. This only applies to words of 5 letters or more, so a short word like "Brot" cannot turn into a different word like "Boot".
5. **Grammar must be exact.** One letter is the whole point of the question: "wohne" versus "wohnt".

## Topic tags and practice tips

- Every question carries a hidden topic tag, for example "verb endings" or "word order".
- **No hints during the test, on any question.** The real exam gives none, so hints would make learners look more ready here than they are on exam day. A learner who cannot do the basics without a hint is not ready either way.
- Tags appear only on the results screen. The 2–3 practice tips come from the tags on questions answered wrong or with "I'm not sure", weakest section first.

## Guardrails against guessing

The main objective is that a learner cannot reach "ready" by guessing.

1. **Typed answers on easy and medium questions.** You cannot guess a German word by luck.
2. **Three real options on hard questions.** A blind guess is right 33% of the time, not 50% as with true/false.
3. **"I'm not sure" on every hard question.** An honest way out instead of a guess.
4. **Negative marking on hard questions only.** Right +22.5, wrong −11.25, so guessing averages to zero.
5. **Typed questions carry 55% of each section.** Luck on the hard questions alone cannot reach 60%.
6. **No answers that are numbers.** Numbers are easy to guess or copy.
7. **No cognates or loanwords.** Nothing can be worked out from English (for example Pizza, Pasta).
8. **No copying from the text.** Reading answers either are not written in the text, or sit next to a wrong option that is.
9. **No hints or topic tags during the test.**
10. **Grammar answers must be exact.** One-letter leniency would accept a wrong form.

## Screens and flow

Three screens, in this order: Start, Questions, Results.

1. **Start:** title "Am I exam-ready?", one line on what it is (15 questions, 3 sections, about 10 minutes), a note that "I'm not sure" is a smart answer, and a Start button.
2. **Questions:** one question per screen. Shows the section name and progress (for example "Grammar, 2 of 5"). Typed questions have a text box, hard questions have 4 buttons. A Next button moves on. No hints.
3. **Results:**
   - Overall score as a percentage, with the verdict: Ready, Almost ready or Not ready
   - Each section as "X of 5 right"
   - A warning for any section below 40%
   - 2–3 practice tips from the topic tags, weakest section first
   - A Retake button

**UI decisions:**

- **No countdown timer.** At A1 learners rarely fail on time, and a ticking clock pushes people to guess. Instead, time is tracked silently: the expected time is about 10 minutes, and anyone who takes over 20 minutes gets a line on the results screen: "You're accurate but slow. Practise under time before you book."
- **Confetti after each completed section, plus a progress bar.** It celebrates finishing a section, not getting answers right, so learners are never pushed to guess.
- **Results only at the end.** No right/wrong feedback during the test.
- **Correct answers are never shown.** With only one question set, revealing answers would let learners memorise them for a retake.
